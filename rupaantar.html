The Problem

Why Rupaantar Exists

🌍 India's Pollution Crisis

India ranks 5th globally in pollution. Crop residue burning causes severe smog and has contributed to 1.5 million deaths from air pollution annually.

🌳 Deforestation

30 million trees are lost annually across India due to paper production. Farmers lack profitable alternatives for disposing of agro-waste.

👩 Underemployed Women Artisans

Rural women artisans remain underemployed and underrecognized, lacking access to markets and formal income streams.

Our Solution

Eco-Paper from Crop Waste

Rice husk and straw - agricultural byproducts typically burned - are pulped and processed by trained rural women artisans into handmade premium eco-paper. The result: a ₹5/unit product that is tree-free, sustainable, and 3-5x cheaper than competitors.

Raw Material
Rice husk and straw sourced from farmers - waste that would otherwise be burned.
Artisan-Made
Rural women trained in paper-making; revenue flows directly to them.
Products
Greeting cards, notebooks, decorative items, stationery - sustainable and high quality.
Target Markets
Corporate gifting, NGOs, retail, D2C online and B2B/Wholesale partnerships.

Since 2022

The Journey of Rupaantar

Rupaantar's story is one of relentless iteration - experimenting with multiple waste streams before arriving at a scalable, impactful solution.

2022
First Iteration
Agarbatti from Waste Flowers
Started with making incense sticks (agarbatti) from temple floral waste. The model faced challenges when beneficiaries shifted to using fresh flowers, making waste supply unreliable. Project pivoted.
2023
Second Iteration
Leather from Orange Peels
Explored bio-leather production using orange peel waste. While innovative, the process proved difficult to scale with available resources and beneficiary capacity. Team kept experimenting.
2025
Third Attempt
Clay from Rice Husk
Began making clay products from rice husk. Faced significant quality issues - the clay kept breaking apart, cracking while drying, and developing unpleasant odors. A drinkware idea was also proposed, but required machinery too expensive to procure.
NOW
Current Phase
Eco-Paper from Rice Husk
The breakthrough. Started prototyping paper-making using rice husk pulp. Conducted 10 prototyping sessions to refine quality, then partnered with Kalpana Paper Factory in Jaipur for expert guidance. First successful products now in market.
2025-26 Season

What Happened This Year

Prototyping Journey

Started with prototyping after shifting to rice husk paper. Conducted a total of 10 prototyping sessions to test pulp consistency, drying time, texture, and quality.
Decided to bring in external expertise - visited Jaipur and finalized a partnership with Kalpana Paper Factory. Procured 100 sheets to study production quality and standards.
Successfully developed a working prototype - handmade eco-paper sheets from rice husk priced at ₹5/unit, with 44.3% going directly to artisans.
